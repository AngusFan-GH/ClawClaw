# Evidence research

Turn the request into a small set of answerable questions. Record the required time range, region, audience, decision, and acceptable source types before collecting material.

Prefer primary sources, official records, original datasets, standards, and first-party documentation. Use independent secondary sources to add context or challenge a claim. Repeated reporting of one original source is not independent confirmation.

For every material conclusion, retain the claim, source owner, publication or update date, location, exact supporting fact, and important limitations. Separate observed facts, interpretations, forecasts, and assumptions.

When sources disagree, explain whether the conflict comes from date, sample, definition, incentives, or methodology. Do not average incompatible figures into false precision.

Deliver a concise conclusion first, followed by an evidence table, important disagreements, unanswered questions, and the evidence that would change the recommendation. Never claim that a source was checked when it was not accessible.
